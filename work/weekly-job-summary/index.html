Hitting 49% more clicks with two lines of copy.

The Weekly Job Summary is a core lifecycle email reaching 550K+ employers per week. It drives ~1,700 lifecycle-spender conversions per month across upsell and reactivation streams. But according to UXR, employers wanted a more action-oriented and personalized subject line.

So we built an A/B test variant incorporating both.

I led the subject line and preheader, from feedback synthesis to the final test copy.

what employers asked for

Likelihood-to-open was low. A concrete subject line would earn the open, not a generic "weekly update."

workshopping the line

My idea

The neutral or better winner criteria were the answer, because if habit kept open rates flat, the change still rolled out as a CX improvement. Later, the results showed employers weren’t confused.

…and the preheader

Our international lifecycle marketing manager offered three alt options, each giving a preview of what to expect from the email. I pulled the most important bits from each into one.

Track your job activity and posts on Indeed
Catch up on your applications and key insights
Job performance and recommended next steps are inside
MY VERSION
Catch up on job activity, key insights, and next steps

what changed

Only the subject line and preheader differed, while the email body stayed the same.
The test adds the two elements employers asked for.

Those who opened the email were far likelier to click. A clearer, action-oriented subject line set accurate expectations of what's inside. Catchier subjects were usually risky at Indeed, but here, it resulted in more clicks.

View candidates rose 4–5× the other main CTAs. The test specifically pushed more employers to review their applicants, the action “check in on your job status” invites. The framing delivered on its promise.

setting new defaults in my first month

Each lift was steady with a clear business impact: 49% more clicks per email. My versions are now the default for Weekly Job Summary campaigns.

Throughout my work, you’ll find me as the first or founding content designer and teaching partners how to work with me. Indeed didn't have email-specific content design, so I made an email-specific framework. The Weekly Job Summary was the most significant campaign I partnered on, and altogether they formed how I structured Indeed’s email content framework, then trained its internal content MCP.
