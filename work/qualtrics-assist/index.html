Personalizing how an AI assistant speaks to CX teams.

The tone menu in Qualtrics Assist’s original design wasn't wrong, but it just wasn’t ours.

Witty. Confident. Empathetic. Six options handed to users that never once looked to Qualtrics for wit. Our users were CX professionals at major companies, seeking deeper insights from customer feedback. Why would they choose a tone we didn’t have?

Other assistants offered options to customize tone, length, and formality. I saw the potential for personalization and later, to align the tool to users' brand voice.

witty
Didn't match a product built for serious, professional work
confident
Implies its opposite exists elsewhere; a bad signal for a tool meant to help people trust their own data
personable, engaging, direct
Voice traits masquerading as tone
empathetic
Only one that held up, but it's standard

AI guidelines from scratch

I ran our existing product voice against NN/g's four tone dimensions. If we weren't funny or irreverent elsewhere in the product, adding a settings option to be either was a trust risk.

personalization sketches

what the team said

I sought feedback from the product scientist, lead designer, and a UX director, alongside a second research pass on how users reacted to informational vs. supportive tone in other AI features (automated summaries, AI-generated comments). The findings were consistent:

OBJECTIVITY >
Users strongly preferred the tool to be objective in speech
SENSITIVE TO TONE
"Cheery" or emotionally charged tones read as irritating
GET TO THE POINT
Summaries should tell them what they need to know, quickly
NO SLANG IN OUTPUTS
Concerns surfaced with the tool handling informal feedback
MEET THEM WHERE THEY ARE
No desire to be forced into one conversational style
